# Coherent service-or-flat: no frozen Gaussian persistence is needed

The old service-or-flat packet assembly used the detailed `H1/H3` sideband
calculus to keep an osculating Gaussian packet under control over its lifetime.
Those theorems remain correct and useful, but the whole-eddy coherent affine
projection now provides a more invariant assembly route.

The key change is conceptual:

> Christ's inverse Young Gaussian is an **eventwise near-extremal shape mark**,
> not a material object which must be assumed to persist as one frozen packet.

The actual material object is the selected physical role together with its
coherent phase-space transfer measure.  Its common low--high transport is defined
by the whole-eddy affine regression.

On a low-cost selected transfer event use

\[
\boxed{

\sqrt{E_H^{phys}}

\[
C(q)=1+\frac q{\sqrt2}+\frac{\sqrt7}{2},

Each term answers a physical question:

- `E_H^phys`: is the **actual positive interscale transfer geometry** flat?

  extremal triad nonconformally?
- `I_K`: how much does resolved deformation vary **across the whole coherent
  eddy**, after the best common affine motion is removed?

These are invariant/covariant flow observables, not packet-coordinate penalties.

## 8. Eventwise profile marking, not persistence

At a transfer-selected slice, Christ's inverse Young theorem gives a near-Gaussian
affine extremizer and the shell theorem gives its critical mass/radius/aspect
information.  That Gaussian may be used as an **analysis mark for this event**.
At the next common causal slice one may re-extract another Gaussian.

The two marks need not be declared the same packet:

- common affine evolution is gauge;
- nested material coherent labels are canonical;
- small covariance representative changes have summable `Xi_cov`;
- actual material-cell changes are relink/fresh/backflow events;
- large covariance/aspect/deformation changes are physical strain/radius/`D_V`
  events.

Thus re-extraction is compatible with single charging.  What remains is to make
this eventwise marking/registration theorem explicit at the continuum selected
transfer measure level.
