# Complex Young parent marking: symbol freezing already contains the phase lift

The inverse-Young ledger has often been phrased first for magnitudes because

\[
|T_m(f,g,h)|
\le m_*T_1(|f|,|g|,|h|).
\]

That is sufficient to identify Gaussian shape, but magnitude proximity by itself
cannot identify spatial modulation or phase.  There is no need to introduce a
new phase-persistence hypothesis.  The existing smooth-symbol freezing theorem
reduces the physical weighted cell to an **ordinary complex-valued Young near
extremizer**, and Christ's stability theorem is already stated for complex-valued
functions.

## 1. Freeze one selected physical scalar/helical cell

After the existing role/polarization selection, consider one scalar complex
trilinear cell.  Normalize

\[
\|f\|_{3/2}=\|g\|_{3/2}=\|h\|_{3/2}=1.
\]

Let the physical multiplier satisfy `|m|<=m_*`, and let `m_0` be its frozen
value on the selected relative cell.  The smooth-symbol theorem gives

\[
|T_m-m_0T_1|
\le \xi m_*A_3,
\]

where, on a cell of diameter `ell`,

\[
\xi=L_M\ell.
\]

Suppose the selected cell has physical weighted efficiency

\[
|T_m|
\ge Rm_*A_3.
\]

Then

\[
|m_0||T_1|
\ge(R-\xi)m_*A_3.
\]

Since `|m_0|<=m_*`, one obtains the crucial inequality

\[
\boxed{
\frac{|T_1(f,g,h)|}{A_3}
\ge R-\xi.
}
\]

No positive lower bound on `|m_0|` is needed.

\[

\]

Thus `(f,g)` itself—not merely `(|f|,|g|)`—is a complex-valued near extremizing
pair for sharp Young convolution.
