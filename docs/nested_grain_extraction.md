# Transfer-selected nested grain extraction

This is an exact theorem for a finite Gaussian atomic model. It is not a
Navier--Stokes regularity proof.

## 1. Gaussian defect

For three Fourier Gaussian packets with centers `kappa_X,kappa_Y,kappa_Z`,
physical centers `x_X,x_Y,x_Z`, and comparable widths, the scalar overlap
factor has the form

\[
\exp[-D(e)^2],
\]

where

\[
D(e)^2=
\frac{|\kappa_X+\kappa_Y-\kappa_Z|^2}{12\sigma^2}
+
\frac{\sigma^2}{3}\sum_{a<b}|x_a-x_b|^2
-
\log R_{\rm width}.
\]

The exact width factor is

\[
R_{\rm width}=(27a_Xa_Ya_Z)^{3/4},\qquad
 a_i=\frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_X^2+\sigma_Y^2+\sigma_Z^2}.
\]

Thus each atomic edge has

\[
w_e=\beta_e e^{-D(e)^2}
\]

with a nonnegative base weight `beta_e`.

## 2. Do not put the moat around a coordinate grid

A fixed Fourier or phase-space grid creates a boundary problem. Instead put
the moat in the scalar defect variable generated by the transfer itself.

At a parent node `P`, let

\[
T_P=\sum_{e\subset P}w_e,\qquad B_P=\sum_{e\subset P}\beta_e.
\]

Choose `L_P` so that

\[
e^{-L_P^2}B_P\le \varepsilon_{\rm tail}T_P.
\]

Divide the interval `[L_P,L_P+M\Delta)` into `M` annuli. One annulus `I_*`
has transfer mass at most `T_P/M`.

Delete `I_*`. Connect vertices using hyperedges below its lower endpoint.
The connected components are the children of `P`.

Every cross-child edge is now of one of two types.

1. It lies in the deleted moat, whose total weight is at most `T_P/M`.
2. It lies above the upper endpoint `U_*`, whose total weight is at most
   `B_P exp(-U_*^2)`.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Cross}(P)
\le
\frac{T_P}{M}+B_Pe^{-U_*^2}
\le
\left(\frac1M+\varepsilon_{\rm tail}\right)T_P.
}
\]

No geometric counting and no hard-grid boundary estimate is used.

## 3. Nestedness

The construction is recursive inside each child. Children are disjoint
subsets of their parent and are never rejoined. Hence it produces an actual
rooted grain tree.

Let `M_j` and `epsilon_j` be the moat-bin and tail schedules. Since retained
child transfers sum to at most the parent transfer, the total cross loss at
depth `j` is bounded by

\[
\left(M_j^{-1}+\epsilon_j\right)T_{\rm root}.
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\sum_j \operatorname{Cross}_j<\infty
}
\]

whenever

\[
\sum_j M_j^{-1}<\infty,
\qquad
\sum_j\epsilon_j<\infty.
\]

For example, `M_j=(j+3)^2` and `epsilon_j=2^{-2j-4}` work.

## 4. Sticky or decoupled

At a node, either the construction returns a single child carrying the
interaction, in which case the block is sticky at that resolution, or it
returns several interaction components with a certified summable cross
error. The transfer-weighted Bellman inequality can then be applied to the
child masses without an uncontrolled remainder.

Thus the decomposition supplies exactly the interface needed by the previous
module:

\[
\text{one nested child}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\text{Bellman branching with summable cross error}.
\]

## 5. Missing PDE bridge

For an arbitrary near-extremal Navier--Stokes block one still has to prove an
atomic Gaussian extraction with controlled synthesis constants. Young
near-extremizer rigidity supplies one dominant Gaussian profile, but a full
iterative extraction and an estimate of the atomic coefficient budget remain
to be proved.
